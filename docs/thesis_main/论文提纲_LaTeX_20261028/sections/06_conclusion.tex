\section{Conclusion}

We present a framework for measuring and aggregating panoramic room layout annotations through two complementary methods. $Q$ evaluates the geometric quality of individual and collective layouts, while paired-corner structural consensus combines distinct-person support and upper--lower observations into a connected layout. Doorway-boundary viewpoints expose incompatible enclosure selections when the camera sits at a room division: annotations cover either adjoining room or span both. This setting exposes disagreement over which adjoining space to enclose.

Our empirical findings establish how annotators shape collective geometry. Early team expansion raises mean quality, whereas later additions chiefly lift the lower tail and tighten consistency within finite candidate pools. Class composition sets an average trend, but specific incoming and retained members determine which turns survive and where boundaries fall. Across room views, personal structural habits recur while local corner choices vary across viewpoints. These findings directly inform team organization and targeted re-annotation: repeatedly supported structures can be audited against the reference, and enclosure disagreements can be isolated for expert review. Together, the framework and findings provide a rigorous basis for building cleaner layout datasets for model training and evaluation.
