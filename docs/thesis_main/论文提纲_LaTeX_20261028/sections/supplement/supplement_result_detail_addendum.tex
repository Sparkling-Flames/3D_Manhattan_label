\subsection*{C19. Extended individual, team and cross-view results}

\paragraph{Individual geometric and labor responses.}
On 201 images shared by C1, C2 and C3, mean individual Q was 64.965, 66.913 and 80.971, respectively. Mean matched-boundary errors were $1.856^\circ$, $1.730^\circ$ and $0.941^\circ$. This error averages absolute upper- and lower-boundary angular deviations at matched corner pairs and differs from footprint boundary distance $S_b$. On the 130 easy images shared by C2 and C3, their turn-count difference was 0.058 while their Q difference was 13.943 points.

Within-person, within-building difficult-minus-easy comparisons gave C3 an additional 2.330 turns relative to C2 across 15 buildings, positive in all 15. The corresponding Q difference was +5.235 points, positive in 9 buildings. Adding geometric features to the difficulty, annotator and source controls reduced held-out-building turn-deficit MSE from 1.744 to 1.163. Reference turn count is among those predictors and also enters the definition of turn deficit; binary simplification MSE and Q MAE are reported alongside it in Supplement D1. Supplement C7 specifies the prediction procedure.

In 100 slower--faster pairs reusing 35 annotators, the slower person's easy-to-difficult advantage changed by $-$0.054 turns and $-$0.029 short edges on average. Positive changes occurred in 53 and 55 pairs, respectively. The joint-profile quality--labor comparison is reported in Supplement D1.

The 6 expert-confirmed controlled Semi tasks yielded 156 opportunities from 26 annotators, with 25 unchanged submissions. The retained ordinary primary-target subset has 138 responses and the issue-specific outcome counts in C11. 3 separate natural-initialization submissions for e9zR4mvMWw7-39 also retained their initial coordinates. The natural initialization had been checked by experts before the experiment. Supplement B20 shows the natural example. In the duplicated-pair example in B8, panorama Z6MFQCViBuw-10, A2 removed the added pair while retaining every other coordinate and A3 retained it.

\paragraph{Small groups, completion and retrospective stability.}
On the fixed 171-image panel, the consecutive changes in mean Q for sizes 3--6, 6--10, 10--20 and 20--30 were +4.74, +1.75, +1.31 and +0.50 after rounding. The corresponding P10 gains were +8.27, +3.73, +3.78 and +2.85; each difference was calculated before rounding the displayed endpoint levels. Actual failure rates rose by 1.894 percentage points from 3 to 6 people and fell by 0.416 points from 20 to 30. Complete-and-evaluable rates changed by $-$1.857 and +0.420 points. An output can be produced but unscorable, accounting for the distinction between these rates.

Of 98 capacity-35 images, 97 enter the jointly scorable 30-to-35 comparison in D2; their within-image standard deviation falls by 1.952. C10 gives the full capacity strata.

Retrospective stability requires the range of image-mean Q from a candidate size through the actual full pool to be at most a specified tolerance, at least $\max(5,\lceil0.15N\rceil)$ additional members to remain observable, and every size to have at least 64 scorable paths. With a 2-point tolerance, onsets were detected on 163 of 206 ordinary images: 123/130 easy, 25/39 moderate and 15/37 difficult images. The remaining 43 had no detected onset. Median sizes among detected onsets were 9, 16 and 17. Tolerances of one, two, 3 and 5 points yielded 145, 163, 170 and 184 detected onsets, respectively. The criterion uses the complete observed curve.

Early-entrant composition tracked later quality within the stored natural paths. Orders beginning with 6 C2+C3 entrants averaged about 1.78 Q points higher over sizes 6--20 than those beginning with C1+C2 entrants, with positive differences on 165 of 190 comparable images. These orders vary in both profiles and particular people. Separately calculated one-to-two and two-to-3 paired changes averaged about +4.23 and +3.61 Q points on the 171-image panel, requiring both endpoints scorable for each contrast. At 2 people, either person's support meets the at-least-half rule.

\paragraph{Retained backgrounds and geometry.}
In the joint-profile background-size comparison in D3, the mean difference in signed effects between sizes is +0.014 points, despite the reduction in absolute change.

The background-reversal comparison fixes image, reference, team size, replacement size and exchanged members, using unchanged endpoints. Of 276 blocks observed under at least two backgrounds, 56 reversed sign across 7 images; 24 crossed both +1 and $-$1 Q point across 5 images. Requiring identical background profile counts yielded 120 subgroups, representing 108 distinct blocks across 7 images and 4 buildings. 18 subgroups reversed sign; 9 crossed both thresholds across 4 images. Blocks have differing numbers of observed backgrounds. The q9vSo1VnCiC-30 example in the main text had the largest effect range among comparisons with the same exchanged members and background profile counts.

Score range and geometric components explain additional response differences. In the 20-person, 7-person C2-to-C3 comparison, 1,157 pairs with both Q values at least ninety differed by 2.064 points on average; 672 pairs with at least one lower value differed by 11.012. An image can contribute pairs to both sets. In a separate 8-person, 4-person C3-to-C1 comparison on jtcxE69GiFV-13, A, B and C scored 72.914, 82.144 and 72.322. B retained 6 selected nodes, while A and C retained 4.

\paragraph{Cross-view structure and quality.}
Across the 128 directly related view pairs, same-person versus different-person mean gaps were 0.671 versus 0.752 effective turns, 0.377 versus 0.445 turn deficits and 0.363 versus 0.414 short-edge counts. Both comparisons use the same common-worker pool within a pair and equal pair weights. Across all pairs, simplification agreement was 88.22\% for the same person and 86.37\% for different people. Uniformly unsimplified responses left many pairs with little variation; the 15 pairs with short edges in both references yielded the larger agreement difference reported in the main text.

Across 4 views of the illustrated room, A40 retained 10, 14, 16 and 16 effective turns, matching the reference counts; A41 retained 8, 4, 9 and 12. Among 18 common annotators in the two illustrated views, the door-side endpoint was matched in both views by 5 people, neither by 6 and one by 7; the vanity-side counts were 5, 8 and 5. The counts agree at azimuth tolerances of 3, 6 and 9 degrees. D4 reports the second room, where local endpoint agreement does not consistently favor the same person.

Same-room mean-Q gaps averaged 5.051 points, compared with 8.931 for controls matched on building, difficulty, reference turns and geometrically hidden reference vertices. The comparison used common workers and 215 directional comparisons involving 113 same-room pairs in 9 buildings. Gaps were smaller in 8 buildings. Control pairs had unknown recorded relation and partially verified physical-room identity.

Within-pair Q rank correlation was positive on 105 of 128 pairs; 40 of 57 people repeated relative-performance direction on more than half their pairs. Raw individual cross-view Q correlation was 0.401, peer-centered correlation 0.199 and correlation after adjustment for building-external personal history 0.013. The image-mean prediction uses both directions of 128 pairs, giving 256 comparisons; C7 defines its target-image and building aggregation and the separate individual-Q and structural prediction estimands.

The full-composition transfer analysis uses 324 panels from 127 directly related ordinary image pairs in 13 buildings. Moments are averaged by direct pair, declared room group and building; aggregated covariance and variances yield the pooled correlation of $-$0.052. The specified replacement instead follows the same retained and exchanged people across 19 view pairs in 7 buildings. C14 reports the jointly scorable event support and pair-level effects.

On the fixed 17 doorway images, screening excludes 145 of 584 submissions and retains 439. The main conditional-mean difference of +1.581 uses each condition's own scorable outputs. Both retain the same whole-space reference and underlying base orders. The complete 3--34-person doorway analysis uses decreasing image support above 14, whereas the main 3--14 curves retain all 17 images.
